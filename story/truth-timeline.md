## Setting

**Humble, West Virginia — autumn 1997.** Humble is a coal town after
the coal: half the storefronts on Main are papered over, everybody works two
towns away or not at all. In 1996 a new controlled-release painkiller —
**Oxytera CR**, made by **Prescott Pharmaceuticals** — arrived in Appalachia
and found soft ground. It is the load-bearing wall of this season
(canon.md, "Humble and Oxytera").

Two pharmacies:
**Reed's Drug** (est. 1939; Sam Reed, honest, old-fashioned) and **Value-Med
Discount Pharmacy** (Main St. since 1996, Rt. 3 Plaza, Beech Fork; managed by
Jonathan Wright). One doctor who matters: **Dr. Winfred "Win" Sparks**,
who has kept Saturday hours since March `attach.board-letter`.

The **river bend off Route 9** — fire road past mile marker 6, gate closed
after dusk, twenty minutes from town by bicycle `web.mapfinder-bend`
— is where Frank taught Casey to skip rocks ("He can still do eleven. My
record is six." `file.english-journal`, `photo.river`). The county has
delayed the guardrail above it three times `web.times-guardrail`.

## The truth (Layer 0)

Dr. Sparks writes generous scripts for cash-paying "patients" from three
counties; Value-Med fills them without questions; Jonathan skims the cash
and keeps the ledger. That is the pipeline.

Casey found the first loose thread on **August 9** — an envelope of cash in
Jonathan's good coat while looking for the truck keys — and pulled on it
quietly: a pill bottle with a stranger's name in the glovebox; a Saturday
waiting room full of out-of-county plates at Sparks' office (**Sep 26**);
finally (**Oct 5**) the ledger page in the den desk, which she copied by hand
and hid in her School folder, "because nobody ever looks at homework."

She had already gone looking for the other end of the pipe. Her mother had
died in the spring of the pills Prescott sells. From the library she
could prove Humble filled more Oxytera than Charleston `cd.oxytera`
— "nobody is going to print any of that. its all legal. you need something
INTERNAL." In early September she found her way onto IRC, and on IRC she
found **nightshift**: an adult, patient, who said he had been building a case
on Sparks for two years and knew about him before she did. He walked her
through defacing Prescott's homepage (**Sep 12–13** ) and, when the
public site turned out to open onto an internal directory, through pulling
what was there: a Q3 territory summary (saved **Sep 14**), a Region 4 field
bulletin naming Sparks, a memo about not saying "addiction" out loud, a
spreadsheet of overdoses by county. She burned one copy onto a disc labeled
like a mix (**Summer_97**) and, on **Oct 9**, PGP-locked the raw dump onto a
floppy behind the one word that was really hers. Prescott took its site down
and kept it down a month.

nightshift's help came with a door. During the operation he had a remote
console into her machine — set up to move files, kept to keep watch —
and through it he read what he should not have: her diary, her songs, her
saved logs, the safe word she and Sadie had minted on Oct 3. When he moved
from IRC to a direct IM line on **Oct 7** and she balked ("i don't KNOW
you"), he proved himself with her own words (**Oct 8**): the unfinished
chorus that was "not ANYWHERE," her dad's eleven skips, and — off the pasted
record — junebug. It terrified her and it worked. She pasted the exchange
into a poems file as insurance ("if i'm ever wrong about him, this is how
you'll know") and wrote in the diary: "he knew junebug. HOW."

She told no one the whole of it. She asked Sadie hypotheticals and promised
her everything "saturday." She asked Sam Reed one careful question at the
counter (**Oct 6**). She kept a spoken diary entry she told herself not to
listen to ("it's not mine to tell"). And on **Oct 9**, in a two-hour session,
she and nightshift arranged to meet: **the bend, 10 PM Friday, bring the page,
come alone, delete the logs**. She starred it in her datebook.

Jonathan, who has always read other people's paper, had been reading *this
machine* since the day he plugged it in at the new house and guessed her
password in one try (**Jun 30**). He read the August entry. He read the
ledger copy. He read the logs and the star. He told Sparks.

**Friday, Oct 10.** Algebra quiz. 3:30 PM, Chad grabbed her arm in the
parking lot over "some guy she talks to at 2am." 4–7 PM, wine coolers with
Angel behind Gene's; home at seven, watched through her own front door. 6:02
PM Chad's apology arrived, unread. 9:12–9:36 PM her last session — the log
where nightshift offers junebug as the word that will tell him it's really
her, tells her to come alone, and signs off "goodnight" mid-sentence. 9:44 PM
she deleted the log — to the Recycle Bin. About 9:45 she rode her bike toward
Route 9.

Around ten, Frank, drunk on his porch, watched a **clean, dark, quiet 4x4**
come up the fire road and stop at the bend with its lights off. Not the
Daniels boy — "somebody who washes his car." `email.ruth.yourdad`

**What happened at the bend is not written**. Season 1 proves whose
hands — Jonathan's, in Sparks' vehicle  — and never what. Whether
nightshift ever reached the bend, or saw the 4x4, is OPEN. Jonathan
took her backpack; the copies in it were copies.

**Without a trace:** she is not found in Season 1. The season's last reveal
is the pipeline, the source, and the house — not the body.

**The mistake that undoes them is the machine.** Home before eleven, Jonathan
sat at her desk and walked her cached pages to learn what she knew. He came
back after the house slept: 1:52 AM the map of the bend, 2:08 the Prescott
product page, **2:14 AM the diary read and deleted**, 2:17 a nine-minute
dial-up, 2:19 the Prescott board, and at **2:31 AM the power cut at the
wall** — no goodbye chime, no trace, he thought. He missed the ledger copy
in the homework, the log in the Recycle Bin, the poems file in Writing!!,
the floppy in the drive, and the disc. And every cold boot since has printed
the hour on the first screen the player ever sees `computer.bootWarning`.

He kept coming back. Every other night at ten to midnight he dialed up from
her machine and read what arrived for her. Angel, watching the buddy
list, saw SunflwrC81 sign on. So did nightshift.

## Act skeleton (feeds the clue graph)

- **Act 1 — "The story doesn't hold":** login → the mundane machine (mail,
  pictures, the D: disc that is mostly mp3s) → the Chad fight and the ★ 10pm
  → Sadie's mail or her chat → *`the-meeting`: she was meeting someone she
  trusted, arranged online.*
- **Act 2 — "The obvious suspects can't be it":** the recovered log and the
  poems file → *`stolen-intimacy`: he had access to her private world* →
  Chad's alibi in his own jealousy → *`chads-window`* → Angel resolves into a
  witness → Frank's clean 4x4 → *`the-clean-truck`: not Chad, not Frank —
  someone with access to her secrets and a car he washes.*
- **Act 3 — "The house":** the ledger copy and Sam's letters →
  *`the-pipeline`* → the remote session → *`the-watcher`* (the player now
  "knows" nightshift is the one who reads her machine) → the floppy behind
  junebug → *`the-source`: Prescott's own paper, with Sparks' name on it* →
  Rebecca → *`who-shaped`: someone who reads your words and answers as you*
  → the diary's properties or the modem log → *`the-house`: 2:14 AM. They
  were already home.* Season ends on the sting.
- **The Prescott thread interleaves, not replaces:** disc texture in Act 1
  (`oxytera notes`, the territory summary reads as noise), motive in Act 2
  (Sam), proof in Act 3 (`the-source`). The player is meant to arrive at the
  end believing nightshift = the house. The text never says so. The season's
  last word — junebug making him run — reads as the thief exposed, and as the
  friend realizing who now has her machine. Both. (who-knows-what.md
  "The double reading.")
