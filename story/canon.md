# Last Login: 1997 — Story Canon

This document establishes current story canon. Treat these points as factual unless explicitly changed later. Do not invent answers to unresolved mystery elements.

## Setting

**Year:** 1997
**Location:** Humble, West Virginia
**Protagonist / Missing Person:** Casey Taylor, 16

Humble is a small Appalachian coal community already struggling economically when a new prescription painkiller begins spreading rapidly through the area.

The story is experienced through Casey Taylor's preserved home computer.

---

## Casey Taylor

Casey is 16, intelligent, curious, technically capable, and more comfortable with computers than most adults around her.

She is not an elite hacker.

She knows enough about:

- HTML
- websites
- basic networking
- directories/files
- early Internet services
- computers generally

to explore, experiment, and occasionally get somewhere she should not.

Casey eventually goes missing.

The central question of the game is:

> **What happened to Casey Taylor?**

---

## Casey's Mother — June

Casey's birth mother was **June Taylor**.

June had been prescribed **Oxytera CR**, a controlled-release opioid manufactured by Purdont Pharmaceutical Company.

She became dependent on the medication and ultimately died from an overdose.

Her death deeply affected Casey and is a major reason Casey becomes interested in Purdont.

This is not an abstract cause for Casey.

It destroyed her family.

**junebug** — Casey's father's nickname for Casey, after her mother: little June. Casey carries it quietly — it is her screensaver word, and her handle online. Most players will meet the word long before they learn what it means.

The sunflowers were June's. The login hint Casey wrote for herself — "moms flower + the year" — means her real mother, whatever the word "mom" means day to day in the house she lives in now.

## Casey's Household — Leslie

The woman in Casey's day-to-day life is **Leslie Wright** — her **stepmother**, whom Casey calls Mom. Casey lives with Leslie and Leslie's husband **Jon**.

Casey's **father** lives elsewhere and calls the house; the relationship is strained but not severed ("if you want to see him this weekend that's fine, tell me first").

Leslie is not a villain and not an obstacle: she is an ordinary, caring guardian who has no idea what is on that computer.

The exact chain of marriages and custody that produced this arrangement is soft canon — implied, never litigated in content.

---

## Humble and Oxytera

Casey's mother is not an isolated case.

By 1997, Humble and surrounding Appalachian communities are beginning to suffer from widespread Oxytera use.

The effects are becoming visible:

- addiction and dependence
- overdoses
- escalating prescriptions
- families destabilizing
- theft and other secondary effects
- children losing parents
- community institutions struggling to understand what is happening

But there is not yet broad public understanding of an opioid epidemic.

People largely see individual failures, individual overdoses, irresponsible patients, or isolated bad doctors.

Casey sees something larger happening.

> **The community is being ripped apart, and nobody seems to be listening.**

---

## Purdont Pharmaceutical Company

**Purdont Pharmaceutical Company**, commonly called **Purdont Pharma**, is the fictional analogue for Purdue Pharma.

Its major new product is:

# Oxytera CR

**CR = Controlled Release**

Oxytera is a powerful controlled-release opioid pain medication.

Purdont publicly presents Oxytera as an important advancement in the treatment of persistent pain.

The company is experiencing enormous commercial success.

Prescriptions are growing extremely quickly.

Purdont is aggressively expanding the market beyond severe or terminal pain and encouraging broader treatment of chronic pain.

---

## What Purdont Knows

Purdont is not a cartoon organization secretly plotting to addict people.

The internal reality is more believable and more disturbing.

Company personnel are seeing warning signs including:

- dependence concerns
- patients requiring escalating doses
- early refill behavior
- reports that relief may not consistently last as long as advertised
- unusually rapid prescription growth
- particularly heavy adoption in Appalachian communities
- physicians expressing concerns about addiction
- misuse reports

At the same time:

- sales representatives are strongly incentivized
- high-prescribing physicians are deliberately targeted
- sales territories are ranked by growth
- Appalachia is viewed as a major commercial opportunity
- internal concerns are frequently reframed as barriers to physician adoption
- leadership does not want fear of addiction to slow Oxytera's growth

The important point is:

> **Purdont possesses information suggesting a serious problem is developing and continues pushing aggressively anyway.**

There should not be one absurd document saying "we know we're killing people."

The truth emerges from many ordinary corporate documents viewed together.

---

## Casey's Initial Attack on Purdont

Casey is angry.

Her mother is dead.

People around Humble are suffering.

Purdont continues presenting itself publicly as a company helping people.

Casey decides to deface Purdont's public website.

This begins primarily as teenage anger and protest, not a sophisticated investigation.

She succeeds in altering or replacing content on Purdont's public homepage with a message criticizing the company and what Oxytera is doing to Humble.

The exact message is TBD.

---

## The Accidental Discovery

While inside Purdont's poorly secured 1997 web infrastructure, Casey discovers something she was never supposed to see.

The company's public web environment exposes a path into an internal employee/staging/intranet system.

Casey follows it.

She suddenly has access to portions of Purdont's internal network.

What began as website vandalism becomes something much more serious.

---

## What Casey Finds

Casey begins discovering and downloading internal Purdont material.

Possible canonical categories include:

- Oxytera sales reports
- prescription-growth reports
- physician targeting lists
- sales representative rankings
- territory performance
- internal marketing presentations
- chronic-pain expansion strategies
- discussions of physician addiction concerns
- dependence-related correspondence
- reports involving early refill behavior
- internal discussions about Appalachia
- documents showing extraordinary Oxytera penetration in West Virginia
- information involving physicians or organizations Casey recognizes from Humble

No individual document needs to prove everything.

The player eventually understands the significance by connecting them.

---

## The Humble Connection

At some point Casey discovers that Humble is not merely suffering coincidentally.

Purdont has specifically identified the Appalachian region as an important growth market.

Internal material shows unusual attention being paid to communities like Humble.

Casey may find local:

- physicians
- prescription volumes
- pharmacies
- sales targets
- sales representative activity
- marketing initiatives

The exact local conspiracy and which Humble residents are implicated remain unresolved canon.

Do not invent those answers yet.

---

## Why Casey Becomes Dangerous

Purdont quickly discovers that its public website has been compromised.

Initially, the company believes this may simply be vandalism.

Then someone realizes that the intrusion went farther.

Internal directories were accessed.

Files may have been downloaded.

Purdont does not necessarily know immediately that Casey Taylor is responsible.

Meanwhile, Casey realizes she has found something much larger than she expected.

She begins:

- saving documents
- researching connections
- talking to friends
- leaving fragments across her computer
- potentially contacting someone she believes can help

Exactly who Casey told, how much she revealed, and whether that decision contributed to her disappearance remain part of the mystery.

## Casey's Computer

Casey's computer therefore contains two overlapping stories.

### Casey's ordinary life

Friends, family, school, photographs, IM conversations, websites, notes, interests, jokes, arguments, relationships, and normal teenage life.

### Casey's secret investigation

Fragments involving:

- Purdont
- Oxytera
- her mother's death
- downloaded documents
- web history
- cached material
- usernames/passwords
- notes
- unusual files
- conversations about what she discovered

The Purdont investigation should initially appear to be only one small part of Casey's life.

The player should gradually realize its importance.

---

## Player Experience

The player should NOT begin the game knowing:

> Casey hacked Purdont and uncovered evidence of corporate misconduct.

That is a major discovery.

Early Purdont references should seem like background material connected to Casey's mother's death and anger toward the company.

The progression should feel more like:

> Casey hated this drug company.

then:

> Casey messed with their website.

then:

> Wait. She got inside.

then:

> She downloaded internal documents.

then:

> Purdont knew someone had accessed them.

then:

> Did they discover it was Casey?

then ultimately:

> Did what Casey found have something to do with her disappearance?

---

## Tom Mercer / Sheriff's Office

Tom Mercer works for the **Humble County Sheriff's Office**.

The player has been given access to a preserved investigative copy of Casey's computer.

Casey's original evidence is read-only.

The added **Case Files** application gives the player:

- notes
- evidence copies
- case information
- messages from Tom

Tom occasionally communicates through Case Files, primarily through text with rare voice messages.

Tom is a guide and investigative contact, not a constant tutorial narrator.

He should not know the solution to Casey's disappearance.

---

## Mystery Rule

Do not prematurely establish who caused Casey's disappearance.

Purdont's conduct, Casey's intrusion, and the missing-person case are connected, but the exact chain of events remains intentionally unresolved.

There should be multiple plausible people or groups who may know more than they admit.

The story should avoid becoming:

> Evil pharmaceutical company sends assassin after teenage hacker.

The actual answer should be more human, complicated, and believable.

---

## Core Theme

The emotional center of the Purdont storyline is:

> **A sixteen-year-old girl recognized what was happening to her family and her town before the adults around her did. Nobody listened. Then she found proof that someone knew more than they were saying.**

That is the canonical foundation for the Purdont/Oxytera storyline in **Last Login: 1997**.

---

## Casey's Offline Backup

Once Casey realizes the Purdont material may be important, she becomes increasingly paranoid about keeping only one copy.

She begins backing downloaded documents up to **CD-Rs**.

To disguise them, Casey labels the discs like ordinary music mixes or copied albums from bands she likes.

Examples:

- `summer mix 97`
- `sadie mix`
- `road songs`
- fictional band/album names
- handwritten track lists that do not match the actual contents

To anyone casually looking through her room, they appear to be ordinary teenage music CDs.

Some may contain actual music alongside hidden or inconspicuously named data folders.

The existence and location of these backup discs can become an important later discovery.

---

## IRC / Hacker Community

As Casey realizes the material is more serious than she first understood, she begins looking for help outside Humble.

She finds her way into IRC channels frequented by programmers, hackers, security hobbyists, and other technically knowledgeable people.

At first her questions are vague:

- how to tell if someone knows you accessed a server
- whether web server logs identify visitors
- how to safely copy files
- whether deleted logs can be recovered
- whether someone can trace an Internet connection

Over time, Casey reveals more.

Her IRC conversations become noticeably more urgent and intense.

She eventually tells a small number of people that she accessed a pharmaceutical company's internal network and found information she believes could prove the company knows its drug is hurting people.

These conversations should feel like real 1997 IRC:

- handles instead of real names
- terse messages
- jokes and sarcasm
- people coming and going
- fragmented conversations
- skepticism
- occasional genuinely useful advice
- some users acting intimidating or reckless

Casey does not know who these people really are.

---

## The Hacker / Future Ally — nightshift

**nightshift is this person.** The ominous presence the existing Season 1 chain builds up — the watcher, the remote access, the deleted files — is the IRC friend Casey met during the Purdont investigation.

To the player: ominous for most of the season. The reveal that nightshift was frightened FOR her, not hunting her, is the recontextualization gut-punch — it must re-read earlier threatening behavior, not merely excuse it.

Current season discovery text was written to be read the wrong way on purpose; a future content pass must make sure nothing states the hostile reading as resolved fact.

One IRC user becomes particularly important.

Initially, both Casey and eventually the player have reason to distrust this person.

Their handle, technical ability, secrecy, and behavior make them an obvious candidate for the threatening "hacker" presence in the story.

They may:

- warn Casey that she has gotten in over her head
- tell her Purdont may have logs identifying her
- help her understand what she downloaded
- encourage her to make offline backups
- disappear from IRC without explanation
- know things they seemingly should not know

Later, this character is connected to the unexpected remote-access events on Casey's computer.

The player initially interprets those events as hostile.

Eventually it is revealed that this person may actually have been trying to **help Casey**, protect what she found, or finish something Casey was unable to complete.

Do not reveal this alignment too early.

The player should spend meaningful time wondering whether the mysterious hacker is:

- helping
- watching
- manipulating the investigation
- connected to Purdont
- responsible for Casey's disappearance

The eventual reveal that the apparent intruder is an ally should recontextualize earlier threatening behavior rather than simply excuse it.

## Resolved canon decisions (2026-08-31)

1. **Leslie Wright is Casey's stepmother** (see "Casey's Household").
   The birth mother, June, died of an Oxytera overdose. All shipped
   Leslie content stands as-is — Casey calls her Mom.
2. **nightshift is the hacker/future ally** (see that section). The
   existing chain's ominous framing is the intended misdirection;
   future content work interleaves the Purdont arc and keeps the
   hostile reading unconfirmed until the reveal.
3. **junebug is canon**: June is the mother; junebug was her father's
   nickname for Casey; Casey made it her screensaver word and her
   handle. NOTE the shipped beat this powers: Sadie's safe-word pact
   ("say junebug and i come get u") and Casey's "he knew junebug. HOW.
   i keep coming back to it. only me and..." — the hostile read is that
   the watcher stole a childhood-private word; the true read is that
   Casey WAS junebug to nightshift all along, and in her fear she
   never connected the two worlds. That line is the reveal's hinge —
   future content must protect its ambiguity.


## Solitaire as a front (seeded 2026-08-31)

The Solitaire on Casey's machine is a real, working game — and a front.

Sending cards of the four foundations (left to right) showing
**3, 2, 4, Ace** — a sequence that cannot occur in normal ascending play, so
you only ever reach it on purpose — opens a hidden login: a bare
green-on-black terminal titled **porthole**. It asks for a username and
password.

Someone taught Casey to hide it this way. The working theory (soft
canon, to be developed): this is how nightshift had her move
files/stay reachable — a dead-drop channel disguised inside the most
innocuous program on the machine, so that even someone sitting at her
desk would see only a card game. "porthole" = a small window into
somewhere else; "a light on" for the night shift.

Current state / open threads (do NOT hard-canonize yet):
- The knock (foundations reading 3,2,4,Ace) and the backdoor are built.
- Credentials: placeholder password only (SERVER-side, story/engine).
  Who set the username, what it is, and whether the player is meant to
  RECOVER it from elsewhere on the machine is unresolved.
- Past a correct login is a holding screen ("no channel is configured")
  — real content is future authoring: this is presumably where
  downloaded Purdont material was staged, or where nightshift reached
  in. Tie-in to the remote-access set-piece and the CD-R backups is
  intended but unwritten.
- How the player LEARNS the knock is unresolved (an IM hint from
  nightshift, a margin note, a diary line — authored later, never a
  glowing tutorial).
