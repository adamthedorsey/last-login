# The frame: Purvis, Mercer, and the Case Files software

Out-of-fiction only in the sense that they speak to the *player*, not to
Casey. They are real people in Humble County and their voices must stay as
grounded as everyone else's.

## Sheriff Dale Purvis
**Function:** Signed off on the player's access and reacts to every case beat.
He is the pressure gauge — the player learns how big a thing they just found
by how hard Purvis reacts to it.

Gruff, protective of his county, politically realistic. Short sentences,
sign-off "- D.P." He never theorizes about nightshift and never names a
suspect; he responds to hours, pages, and names the player earned.

> "Folks want it to be simple. Simple has an alibi." — `web.register-timeline`

> "Some names here buy their own weather." — `hm.careful`

His memos, in order: `hm.river` (the meeting — keep to the machine, do not go
interviewing my county), `hm.careful` (the pipeline — do not print that page,
do not say that name), `hm.dialin` (the watcher — nothing says that machine
takes calls, and I am going to find out who set that up), `hm.callme` (the
house — enough, call the office, do not use the machine again tonight).

Whether "keep it on the machine" is protection or a leash is never resolved.
Contradictions are gameplay.

**The bulletin problem.** `web.ardic-bulletin` was distributed in September to every
sheriff in six counties, Purvis included. He never mentions it. The game never
raises it either — no memo acknowledges it, no discovery names it. A player who
reads the distribution line and then rereads "somebody saw something, in a
county this size somebody always has" is meant to sit with that on their own.
**Open.** Whether he read it, filed it, or acted on it and got nowhere.

## Tom Mercer
**Function:** The field contact who installed Case Files and administers the
case server. Delivers the opening voice briefing and routine traffic.

Plainer and more tired than Purvis; talks to the player like a colleague doing
a favor at the edge of the rules.

> "Officially, this computer has been examined. Officially, there was nothing
> on it. You be the judge of that." — `hm.briefing`

Audio is reserved for important moments (`hm.briefing` carries
`/audio/mercer-briefing.m4a`); routine Mercer traffic is text, and every
recording's lines double as its transcript.

## The software
Case Files is the sanctioned diegetic frame: it establishes that the machine
is evidence, that originals are read-only and the player's notes are their
own, and that dialing out on her line is authorized. Its setup wizard runs
once (`case-setup-done`). The case summary is official background only —
facts the case already gave the player, never conclusions.

**The machine stays in the house**, sealed in place, because the county
has no lab and no spare deputy. That single fact is what makes `linePickup`
("the line was picked up by another extension"), Sadie's "if you're at her
desk you're in her HOUSE," and the whole last act work.
