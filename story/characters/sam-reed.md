# Samuel "Sam" Reed, R.Ph.

**One line:** One of the two pharmacists in town, fifty-eight years behind a
counter, who wrote the state board about the script pattern and got no answer.
**Function:** The honest counterweight, and the adult who confirms Casey was
right. Path B to `the-pipeline`.

## Who they are
Runs Reed's Drug, est. 1939. Watched the pattern from his own window since
March and put his name to a letter he knew would cost him. Casey stood at his
counter on Oct 6 and asked him, carefully, "if a doctor writes too many
prescriptions, who checks?" He gave her the short answer. He has not slept
well since she disappeared.

He is the season's moral floor: proof that an adult *did* listen, too late and
too quietly.

## Voice
Formal, old-fashioned, complete sentences, addresses her as "Dear Casey."
Precise about his trade and unsparing about himself. Gets plainer in the
second letter — the whole character is the distance between the two.

> "The short answer. The state board. I should have given you the long one,
> which is: nobody, child. Nobody checks." — `email.sam.question`

> "she was sixteen, and she was the only one in this county doing my job."
> — `email.sam.plain`

## What they know
Partial on S1 — he can describe the machine precisely without being able to
name the doctor in writing. Partial on S2: he knows a girl asked him the right
question and vanished eight days later.

## On the machine
`email.sam.question` (Oct 17, careful), `email.sam.plain` (Oct 18, plain —
grants `the-pipeline`), `attach.board-letter` (the Sept 8 letter itself).

## Open
Whether he ever testifies. Whether the board answers.
