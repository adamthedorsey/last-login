# Season 1: Without a Trace — who knows what

> LAYER 2. The engine of the mystery: every reveal is a secret surfacing in
> the wrong mouth. Keep this table true while writing or the mystery leaks.

## The secrets

- **S1 — The pill pipeline.** Sparks writes, Value-Med fills, Jonathan skims.
- **S2 — Casey knew.** She found the cash, the bottle, the ledger page; she
  copied the page onto the PC and hid it in her homework.
- **S3 — The meeting was arranged online.** nightshift set the time, the
  place, and the terms: come alone, bring the page, delete the logs.
- **S4 — Who nightshift is.** [OPEN — ] The IRC ally who taught her the
  intrusion. An adult; two years into his own case against Sparks; technical
  enough to hold a console open on her machine. **Season 1 never answers
  this**, and every piece of evidence about him is built to read two ways
  (see "The double reading").
- **S5 — What happened at the bend.** Jonathan's hands; Sparks' vehicle and
  cover-up. Shove or plan is **not written**.
- **S6 — Frank saw the 4x4** that night and has stayed silent (drunk, scared
  of his record, certain no one believes him).
- **S7 — Angel's real evening** (wine coolers behind Gene's; her first
  statement was a lie to protect her cousin's job).
- **S8 — Chad's alibi hole** — during the meeting window he was on a barstool
  at Gene's writing an apology and calling her house from the bar phone.
- **S9 — The word "junebug."** Frank's name for Casey, after June. Sadie
  reminted it Oct 3 as their safe word and believed only two people on Earth
  had it. Casey also used it as her IRC handle and set it drifting across her
  screen saver every night, and locked the floppy with it. **Four lives, one
  word** — that gap is the season's central ambiguity.
- **S10 — Leslie's willful blindness.** She saw the new money and chose the
  question she didn't ask.
- **S11 — Jonathan's pattern.** He reads other people's private words and
  answers *as them*. Rebecca lived it, 1989–91.
- **S12 — The 2:14 AM login.** Someone used Casey's PC hours after she
  vanished, read the diary, deleted it, and killed the power at 2:31 AM
  instead of shutting down. The BIOS prints that stamp on every cold boot:
  the player's first screen holds the finale's timestamp, meaningless until
  the diary's properties give it teeth.
- **S13 — Jonathan has been reading this machine since June 30** — the
  password he guessed in one try, the diary, the saved logs, the starred
  datebook. It is how he knew about the meeting, and how a man who never
  spoke to nightshift can look exactly like him. *(New: this is what
  the old S4 was doing wrong.)*
- **S14 — nightshift's door.** He kept a remote console into the machine
  from the Prescott operation, and read her private files through it — the
  unfinished chorus, the eleven skips, the safe word. His "ways of knowing
  things" were literal. He is an ally who violated her, which is the
  point.

## The double reading (the season's load-bearing ambiguity)

Every clue about nightshift must support **both** of these, and shipped text
must never settle it:

| The player is meant to conclude | What is actually true |
|---|---|
| nightshift knew her private world because he lives in her house and reads her machine. | nightshift knew her private world because he had a console into her machine (S14). Jonathan reads it too (S13) — separately, at the same time. |
| Rebecca's letter identifies him: Jonathan becomes the person you trust. | Rebecca's letter is entirely true about Jonathan and points at the wrong crime. He never wrote a word as nightshift; he read what nightshift wrote. |
| The remote session is the watcher coming back for what he wants. | It is nightshift, dialing in to check on her and finding the diary gone — the moment he learns the house cleaned the machine. |
| "he knew junebug. HOW. only me and s." — he stole the safe word. | He always called her junebug; it was her handle. She never connected the two worlds, and neither does the player. |
| The bend was a trap set by nightshift. | The bend was a meeting nightshift set. **Jonathan read about it** and got there first. |

The reveal, when a later season lands it, must **re-read** this behavior, not
excuse it: he watched a sixteen-year-old through a door he installed, and it
still did not save her.

Rules that keep the ambiguity alive:
1. No shipped line may state that nightshift IS Jonathan. None does today.
2. No shipped line may state that he isn't, or name him.
3. Discovery text names the *shape* of a thing (access, a pattern, an hour),
   never the person. Check any new discovery against `the-watcher` and
   `who-shaped`, which do this correctly.
4. Purvis and Mercer never theorize about nightshift. They react to hours,
   pages, and names the player has actually earned.

## Reveal moves (secret → wrong mouth → discovery)

A secret is inert until it appears where it shouldn't.

1. **S9 in nightshift's mouth** — the recovered Oct 10 log offers junebug as
   the word that proves it's her; Sadie's Oct 3 log shows only two people had
   it. → `stolen-intimacy`: whoever he is, he had access to their private
   world. (True in both readings. That is the design.)
2. **S3 vs. Chad's jealousy** — his angry words prove he didn't know who; a
   guilty man isn't jealous of himself. → the exonerating clue hiding inside
   the incriminating scene. → `chads-window`.
3. **S6 vs. S8** — Frank saw a *clean dark 4x4*; Chad's lifted truck can be
   heard from the church. One sighting exonerates both public suspects. →
   `the-clean-truck`.
4. **S11 in Rebecca's mouth** — "ask how that somebody knew what to say." →
   converts `stolen-intimacy` from *how* into *who-shaped*, and hands the
   player the season's wrong answer with total confidence.
5. **S2 surfacing via Sam** — the forwarded board letter and "you asked me a
   question" give the motive spine the ledger copy then proves. →
   `the-pipeline`.
6. **S1 in Prescott's own mouth** — behind her own secret word, the field
   bulletin rewarding Sparks and the memo about not saying "addiction." The
   pipeline doesn't start at the pharmacy. → `the-source`.
7. **S14 as an event** — the remote console opens on its own, goes straight
   to `personal stuff`, asks for one file that is gone, says goodnight. →
   `the-watcher`: it was never magic, it was access.
8. **S12, the machine itself** — modified dates after Oct 10, the half-emptied
   bin, the diary's 2:14 AM. → `the-house`. End of season.
9. **S7's decoy arc** — Angel's changed statement makes Act 2 look like it's
   about her; her apology resolves her into a witness. The game exonerates as
   often as it accuses.

## The frame (out-of-fiction characters) — canon

- **Sheriff D. Purvis** — signed off on the player's access; reacts to case
  beats through Case Files memos (river, careful, dial-in, call-the-office).
  Gruff, protective of his county, aware some names "buy their own weather."
  He never speculates about nightshift.
- **Tom Mercer** — the field contact who installed Case Files and administers
  the case server. Delivers the opening voice briefing `hm.briefing`, `briefing.m4a`. Routine traffic is text; audio is reserved
  for important moments. The old READ FIRST ground rules live in the setup
  wizard pages.
- **The machine stays in the house** — sealed in place because the
  county has no lab and no spare deputy. That is what makes the line pickup,
  Sadie's "if you're at her desk you're in her HOUSE," and the sting work.
- **Audio still to record** (graceful "Cannot play this file." until supplied):
  `public/audio/band-practice.m4a` (~12s, a garage Solar Flare cover falling
  apart, laughter) and `public/audio/dont-listen-to-this.m4a` (~38s, Casey's
  one spoken diary entry — "it's not mine to tell"). Transcripts live in the
  items and must carry the scene alone.

## The Prescott thread

The pharma arc lives in `canon.md`: Prescott Pharmaceuticals and Oxytera CR,
the defacement, the accidental intrusion, the downloads, the discs labeled as
mixes, IRC, and the ally. Prescott took its public site down after the
intrusion and did not restore it until October 14, which is the readable tell
that they audited what happened.

Summer_97 (D:) carries six traded mp3s, a mundane territory-summary fragment,
`cd.oxytera`, and `cd.todo`. The CD-R is Casey's own; she burned it and
labeled it like a music disc.

**Open.** Whether Prescott ever traced the intrusion to Casey, and whether
anyone from the company ever spoke to anyone in Humble. Do not connect them
directly — "evil pharma sends assassin after teenage hacker" is the one story
`canon.md` forbids.
